Broken key extraction

A key that snaps in a door or ignition doesn't mean a new lock or a tow truck. We remove the broken piece and cut a fresh key on site, most often in under an hour.

Signs you need extraction, not replacement

  • A visible metal fragment stuck in the cylinder
  • The key turns but won't come back out
  • The lock feels gritty or resists turning at all
  • You're holding two pieces of what used to be one key

How the fix works

1

We assess the break over the phone

Tell us where the fragment is — car door, ignition, front door, padlock — and we bring the right extraction tools instead of guessing on arrival.

2

The fragment comes out without forcing the cylinder

Using broken key extractors and, when needed, a fine hook pick, we remove the piece without damaging the pins inside the lock.

3

We cut a new key on the spot

Once the cylinder is clear, we cut a replacement from your remaining key or by decoding the lock directly. For transponder car keys, we also program the chip so the vehicle starts.

4

We test it before we leave

The new key gets tested through a full lock and turn cycle, so you're not calling back an hour later.

Frequently asked

Will this damage my ignition or door lock?

In the large majority of cases, no — extraction removes the fragment without harming the pins or tumblers. If the cylinder itself is worn out or damaged from the break, we'll tell you before doing any replacement work, and price is confirmed with you first.

Do you need my car's registration or ID?

Yes. For automotive key work we confirm ownership on site — this protects you as much as us. Bring your registration or ID if the vehicle isn't in your name.

Can you program a key fob, not just cut a mechanical key?

Yes, most transponder and remote fobs can be cut and programmed roadside. A few newer vehicle models require dealership-level programming — we'll confirm this over the phone before dispatching.

What if the break happened inside my house lock?

Same process — we extract the fragment from a deadbolt or padlock and cut a new key, or rekey the lock entirely if you'd like a fresh key code.
